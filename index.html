Why it exists: a business is a hundred small handovers a day.

Something arrives at your door. It is counted, moved, worked on, sold, delivered or written off. A payment lands, or someone says it did. Every one of those is a moment where goods, services or money change hands, and every one of them is invisible until the figures are short at the end of the month.

Relynked puts a name and a time on every one of them, so a gap belongs to one handover between two people rather than to a total nobody can unpick.

How it works: things move. So does responsibility.

Each step is recorded by the person who did it, with a name and a time.

Receive

What arrives is recorded as it arrives. That record is the receipt.

Request

Whoever needs it asks. The kitchen asks the store for tomatoes.

Hand over

The receiver counts it and accepts it. Responsibility moves at that moment.

Deliver

The work gets done and reaches the customer, visible to whoever is waiting.

Reconcile

Both ends count blind and each answers to its own record.

Built for Nigeria, around how business here actually runs.

A screenshot proves nothing. The order stays pending until a named person checks the bank and confirms the money.

Say what came in, in plain words. "From Mile 12: 5 crates tomatoes 24k each." It becomes the receipt.

The till and waiter screens save each order on the device first and send it when the network is back.

Credit goes to a registered customer, with a limit a manager sets. Nobody opens an account for the man in the blue shirt.

Remy answers your customers in English or Pidgin, in the way they wrote to you.
