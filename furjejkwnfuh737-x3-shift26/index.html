Divine,

This is the page that makes the whole magazine defensible in a staff meeting. Everything else is culture; this is real education at the level students are examined at, with the answer overleaf.

Show this page when you ask a lecturer to be staff adviser. It is why they will say yes.

Case one

The answer

Severe pre-eclampsia, showing the signs that come immediately before an eclamptic fit.

Blood pressure at or above 160/110 with proteinuria after 20 weeks, plus the warning signs: frontal headache, visual disturbance, brisk reflexes with clonus, oedema of the face and hands.

The thing to notice is that she walked in, apologising. Severe pre-eclampsia does not arrive on a stretcher. It arrives politely.

01

Do not send her home and do not leave her alone. Escalate to the doctor and the nurse in charge now.

02

Left lateral, quiet room, low light, cot sides up, suction and oxygen within reach.

03

IV access, bloods, strict fluid balance with an hourly urine output chart.

04

BP every 15 minutes, plus reflexes, respiratory rate, urine output and fetal heart.

Magnesium sulphate for seizure prophylaxis, on the unit's regimen. Before the first dose three things must be at the bedside: calcium gluconate 10%, a way of checking reflexes, and a way of counting respiratory rate and measuring hourly urine output. Those three observations tell you toxicity is starting, in that order.

Antihypertensives lower the pressure. Magnesium stops the fit. Two different jobs, both prescribed. Reviewed by two clinical instructors. Written for revision, not for practice.

01 What I got wrong the first time

You said it looked like a disaster and you were right. The first version had four colours, four typefaces, coloured panels changing every page, decorative blobs standing in for photographs, and 46 pages of it. Every page was shouting, so nothing landed.

The magazine you sent me works for the opposite reason. It is disciplined. Look at it again and count: two colours, one typeface family, one handwritten word per spread, and photographs doing everything else. That is the whole trick, and it is a trick you can actually hold to for 96 pages.

02 The four rules

If you take nothing else from this, take these. Written down, given to whoever designs it, and enforced.

Rule one: two colours, and only two

Charcoal and one acid yellow. White is the paper, not a colour. The yellow appears maybe five times on a page: one rule, one highlighted number, one script word. On the awards page it fills a single block, once, because that award earned it. Nothing else in the book gets a coloured background.

Rule two: one typeface

Everything is one grotesque, doing its work through weight and size. Headlines are light and mixed case, which is what makes a page read as mature instead of loud. Labels are tiny, bold, and widely letter-spaced. The only other face in the book is the handwriting.

Rule three: one handwritten word per spread

"The year we survived". "The Lecturer Dictionary". "What's your call". One word, never two, always the emotional word rather than the functional one. This is the single thing readers will recognise in 2027 before they read a sentence.

Rule four: white space is not wasted space

The reason the reference looks expensive is that roughly a third of every page is empty. Resist filling it. A page with six things on it and room to breathe beats a page with fourteen things every single time.

03 Why the photographs are written, not drawn

Every picture slot in this mock-up holds a brief instead of a placeholder image. That was deliberate, and I think you should keep working this way even after the real photographs exist.

A brief says what to shoot, who is in it, where the light comes from, how tight to crop and what the frame is for. Hand page 62 to whoever has the camera and they know they need one room, one queue, ninety seconds a person and sixty-four identical portraits. No discussion.

A fake image tells you nothing. A written brief tells the photographer exactly what to go and take.

Two briefs in here are quietly doing structural work and I would keep both. Photo 03 is the empty lecture theatre at 7:41am that opens the year feature. Photo 84 is the same room, same lens, same height, on the last day after everyone has gone, and it closes the book. The reader recognises it without being told. That is one extra photograph and about four minutes of someone's time.

04 Where the content comes from

You are not writing a magazine. You are collecting one. One form, six questions, one QR code on every noticeboard and class group, running all year.

  1. Something you have said out loud that only a nursing student would say.
  2. A thing a lecturer always says, and what it actually means. No names.
  3. A confession. Anonymous. Nothing about another student, nothing about a patient.
  4. A clinical moment that humbled you.
  5. A photograph, meme, poem or drawing.
  6. Your vote in the Most Likely To categories.

That one form feeds six sections. Editing then becomes selection rather than authorship, which is the only version of this job a student team can actually finish.

05 Nine people

  • Editor. Owns the deadline and says no. One person, not a committee.
  • Four feature editors. One each: the year, lore, the people, the future.
  • Art director. Owns the four rules above and refuses anything that breaks them.
  • Photographer. Owns every brief in the book. On this design, the most important role after the editor.
  • Illustrator. Draws the handwriting and any diagrams. Pay them.
  • Advertising lead. Owns the rate card and the money. Never the editor.

Plus two you borrow: a staff adviser (one lecturer, recruited in week one, who opens doors) and two clinical instructors who sign off the case on page 44.

The one financial rule: take pre-orders before you print, and print what is paid for plus fifteen percent. Magazines do not fail because nobody wants them. They fail because 600 copies were printed on faith and 300 are still in a box in April.

08 What to tell the printer

  • Trim: 210 x 275mm portrait. Extent: 96 pages plus a 4-page cover, and it must be a multiple of four.
  • Text stock: 120gsm matt. Heavier than usual, because a two-colour design with this much white space shows through cheap paper and that ruins it.
  • Cover: 300gsm, matt lamination. Binding: perfect bound, so the spine can be read on a shelf.
  • Bleed: 3mm all round, 12mm clear inside the spine.
  • Proof: one printed proof before the run, checked on paper.

Check the proof for three things: whether the charcoal pages print as charcoal or as muddy grey, whether the yellow prints as sharp as it looks on screen, and whether any text is sitting closer than 12mm to the spine.

09 The rules that keep you out of trouble

  • No lecturer is ever named in a humour section. Phrases only, submitted independently by three or more students.
  • Staff read the Dictionary before it prints and may pull any entry without giving a reason.
  • Nobody is nominated without consent. Anyone may decline; the category prints as withheld, and you print that.
  • Nothing identifying a patient, ever. "A man in his sixties" is the limit. Clinical photographs are staged and captioned as staged.
  • Two readers on every confession, either may veto, and the screening policy prints on the page.
  • Counselling unit contact on any spread dealing with students in difficulty, every edition.
  • Paid features marked as paid, on the page, every time.
  • Two clinical instructors sign the case before it prints.

Affection and mockery look identical in a draft and completely different in print. When you cannot tell which one a page is, it is mockery. Cut it.

10 Do these five things this week

  1. Lock the name and the theme line. Everything else follows from them.
  2. Write the four design rules on one sheet of paper and give it to whoever will design the book. This is the step that stops version one happening again.
  3. Build the six-question form and put its QR in the class groups today. The content problem is solved by time, and time starts now.
  4. Ask one lecturer to be staff adviser. Show them page 44. That page is why they say yes.
  5. Find the photographer. On this design they matter more than the illustrator, because every page is either type or a photograph and there is nothing else to hide behind.

One last thing.

Every idea in here is worth less than the habit of doing it again. A good one-off magazine is a nice memory. A slightly worse magazine that arrives every December becomes the thing the department is known for.

So build edition one so that edition two is easy. The four rules, the flat plan, the printer, the rate card, the form. Write all five down and hand them to somebody in 300 level before you graduate.
