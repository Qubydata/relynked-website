What you would be working on

Software that tells a
business the truth
about itself.

Relynked measures what actually happens inside a business: what came in, what moved, who signed for it, what was sold, and where the two stop agreeing. We started with bars and restaurants in Nigeria and we are building outward from there, into retail, pharmacy, hotels and anything else that moves goods and money.

The work is unusually concrete. You will not be optimising a funnel. You will be standing in a kitchen at nine at night watching someone use the thing you built, finding out it is wrong, and fixing it before Friday.

Honest version

Five things that are
true about working here.

01

We are early

Small team, real customers, not much money. If you need a large organisation around you to do your best work, this will frustrate you and we would rather say so now.

02

You will meet the users

Everyone goes to site. Engineers, designers, everyone. You cannot build for a storekeeper you have never watched work, and we do not let people try.

03

Opinions need reasons

Seniority does not win arguments here. Bring the reasoning and you will be heard on day one. Bring only the conclusion and you will be asked for the rest.

04

Built in Nigeria, for here first

Not a foreign system with Naira signs pasted on. Power cuts, transfers that may never land, buying with no paper. If that context excites you rather than worries you, good.

05

We ship

Ideas are cheap and we have plenty. What we value is the person who takes one and puts it in front of a real user this month.

The bar

What actually gets
our attention.

We have no degree requirement and no minimum years. We do have a bar, and this is it, stated plainly so you can judge yourself against it before spending your time.

01

You have made something

Anything. A product, a business, a tool for your own use, a piece of writing that changed someone's mind. Finished beats impressive. We would rather see a small thing that works than a large thing that was nearly done.

02

You noticed something nobody asked you to

The most useful people we have worked with share one habit: they see the broken thing everybody else stopped seeing, and then they go and fix it without being told.

03

You can explain your reasoning

Not just what you decided, but why, and what you would have needed to see to decide otherwise. This matters more to us than any specific skill, and it is the hardest thing to fake.

04

You are honest about what you do not know

We are building something where a wrong number costs somebody real money. Confidence without calibration is a liability here, not a strength.

Show us something you made.

A link to anything: a repo, a site, a design, a document, a shop you ran. If it lives offline, describe it in one line instead. Leave it blank only if you truly have nothing to point at.

Tell us about something you built, fixed or figured out that nobody asked you to.

This is the question we actually read. What was broken, what you did, and what happened after. Plain words, no polish needed.
