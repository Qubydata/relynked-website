How it works

Goods move. Services get done. So does responsibility.

Every handover is signed by both sides at the same moment. Custody moves when the receiver approves it, not when the giver says it left.

01
ReceiveWhat arrives is recorded as it arrives. That record is the receipt, because most buying here has no paper.Retail · A pallet arrives from the distributor and is checked in at the bay.
02
RequestWhoever needs it asks for it. One list, many lines, one signature.Hospitality · The kitchen asks the store for tomorrow's tomatoes.
03
Hand overThe giver brings it. The receiver counts it in front of them and signs. Responsibility moves at that moment, not before.Hotel · Housekeeping signs for the linen in front of the storekeeper.
04
DeliverThe work gets done and reaches the customer, with every stage visible to whoever is waiting on it.Workshop · The repair is completed and the vehicle is released to the owner.
05
ReconcileBoth ends count blind and each answers to its own record.Pharmacy · The dispensary counts blind against what the system expected after sales.
RequestedWhat was asked for

Signed by whoever asked.

IssuedWhat actually left

Signed by whoever released it.

ReceivedWhat actually arrived

Signed by both, at the point of handover. When these three differ, the gap has two names and a time on it.

Two reconciliation points

A count that can see the answer is not a count.

Both ends of every handover count blind. You enter what is actually there before the system shows what it expected. Then the gaps appear, together, each one a permanent line with a name on it.

The source

received − issued = counted

What came in through your door, less what was handed on to the people who use it, should be what is left in the store. Anything else is a gap, and it has a name on it.

The point of use

received − used − waste = counted

What was signed for, less what the system expected to be used for what you sold, less what was written off, should be what is left at close. Anything else is a gap, and it has a name on it.

Built for Nigeria

Designed around how business here actually runs.

Not a foreign system with Naira signs pasted on.

Keeps working through a power cut

Every device saves locally first. The record catches up when the generator does. Nobody waits for a spinner mid-shift.

A transfer is not money until it lands

A screenshot proves nothing. The bill stays pending until a named person opens the bank app and confirms the credit.

Market buying with no paper

Say what came in, in plain words. "From Mile 12: 5 crates tomatoes 24k each." It becomes the receipt.

Batches, not single units

Where you produce in batches, the system shows what is outstanding across every order at once, so the person making it knows whether to start another run.

Credit belongs to a person, never a place

Credit goes to a registered customer with a code and a standing. Nobody opens an account for the man in the blue shirt.

Your data stays in Nigeria

Personal and transaction data is stored in-country. Your devices are set up and locked to the job, so nothing leaves through a browser.
